# Dietary Choices & Food Footprint Reduction

## Carbon Intensity of Diets
Dietary greenhouse gas emissions vary substantially based on meat consumption. On average, high meat diets generate over 108 kg CO2 equivalent per month per person, whereas omnivorous diets average 83 kg, vegetarian diets average 55 kg, and plant-based vegan diets average 46 kg.

## Livestock & Meat Industry Emissions
- **Ruminant Meat Production**: Beef and lamb production require vast land, feed, and water resources, producing methane during enteric fermentation. Producing 1 kg of beef generates over 60 kg of CO2 equivalent emissions.
- **Meatless Days**: Transitioning to plant-based meals 2-3 days a week reduces dietary carbon footprint by nearly 25% without requiring full dietary conversion.

## Food Waste & Local Sourcing
- **Methane from Landfills**: Decomposing organic food waste in anaerobic landfills releases methane (CH4), a greenhouse gas 28 times more potent than CO2. Composting household scraps diverts waste and produces nutrient-rich soil enrichment.
- **Seasonal & Regional Food**: Consuming locally harvested seasonal produce eliminates long-distance refrigerated freight transportation ("food miles") and excessive single-use plastic packaging.
