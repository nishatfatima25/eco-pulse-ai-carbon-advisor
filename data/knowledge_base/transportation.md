# Transportation Carbon Emissions & Reduction Strategies

## Overview
Transportation accounts for nearly 20-30% of individual carbon footprints in urban areas. The primary sources of emissions are personal gasoline/diesel motor vehicles, short-distance solo flights, and inefficient commuting patterns.

## Personal Vehicles & Maintenance
- **Tire Pressure Optimization**: Maintaining proper tire inflation improves fuel efficiency by up to 3%. Under-inflated tires increase fuel consumption and wear out tires 15% faster.
- **Aggressive Driving & Speeding**: Hard acceleration, rapid braking, and driving over 60 mph (100 km/h) reduce fuel economy by 15% to 30% on highways.
- **Vehicle Weight Reduction**: Removing unnecessary cargo from the boot/trunk saves approximately 1% in fuel efficiency for every 45 kg (100 lbs) removed.

## Sustainable Commuting Alternatives
- **Public Transit Integration**: Switching from a single-occupancy vehicle to bus or urban rail reduces personal daily commute emissions by 45% to 75%.
- **Active Mobility (Bicycles & Walking)**: For journeys under 3 km, walking or cycling produces zero operational carbon emissions and eliminates parking congestion.
- **Electric Vehicles (EVs)**: Transitioning from an Internal Combustion Engine (ICE) vehicle to a battery electric vehicle reduces lifetime emissions by 50% to 70%, even when accounting for current power grid energy mixes.

## Flight & Long-Distance Travel Impact
- **Short-Haul Flights**: Aviation produces approximately 242 grams of CO2 per passenger-kilometer for short-haul flights due to high fuel burn during take-off and landing.
- **Rail Alternatives**: Replacing a 500 km domestic flight with high-speed or intercity rail cuts travel emissions by over 80%.
